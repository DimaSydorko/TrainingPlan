## Done
Approach: **D3 rewrite**. Nothing keeps JS alive in the background any more. The player position is derived from timestamps and re-synced when the app returns. Notifications carry the cues while the phone is locked.

- **Pure schedule** `app/Screens/PlayingScreen/playbackSchedule.ts`:
  - `buildSegments` flattens the workout into one segment per (exercise, lap). Each segment's duration is `breakTimeInSec`. A segment is `manualStart` when its exercise has `repeats`, which mirrors the old `onChangeTimer` rule that pauses before strength sets.
  - `Clock = { segmentIndex, startedAt, pausedAccumulatedMs }`.
  - `advance()` answers "where should the workout be at `now`": it crosses auto segments at their exact end times, stops at a manual-start segment, and finishes after the last one.
  - `upcomingBoundaries()` and `buildCues()` give the notification schedule and its texts.
  - Unit tests are in `app/Screens/PlayingScreen/__tests__/playbackSchedule.test.ts` (17 cases: pause/resume, skip, multi-segment catch-up, finish, pause points, empty workout, cue ids). They passed under a throwaway jest-compatible shim (see Verification), not real Jest.
- **`usePlaying.tsx`**:
  - `playing` and `isPlaying` still decide *where* the player is. A clock ref decides *when*.
  - The UI tick (250 ms) runs only while the app is in the foreground, playing, and the workout isn't finished.
  - Segment completion and the spoken 10/3/2/1 countdown now come from this tick, not from `CountdownCircleTimer.onComplete` / `onUpdate`. This also fixes an old stale closure: the countdown used to check the *first* exercise's break time.
  - After an auto-advance, the next segment starts at the exact boundary time, so there is no drift per segment.
  - **Background resync:** on `AppState` → `active`, `advance()` runs and the player jumps straight to the right segment. It skips the sound, TTS and vibration of the boundaries it crossed, but still records the running approach's entered values (`onApproachUpdate`).
  - If the workout finished in the background, `isWaitForSubmit` is set and the "Workout complete" sound and TTS are skipped (`isCompletedSilently`).
  - New return values (additive): `remainingSec`, `clockVersion`, `timerSync`, `isCompletedSilently`, `getSchedule`.
- **`PlayingScreen.tsx`**:
  - The countdown circle is now only a visual. It is remounted with `initialRemainingTime` after a resync or a mid-segment start (`timerSync`).
  - The remaining-time text and `TimeObserver`/`PlayHeader` use the clock's `remainingSec`.
  - `<BackgroundAction>` is replaced by `useBackgroundCues`.
- **`components/BackgroundAction.tsx` deleted.** Its job moved into `app/services/backgroundSession.ts` and `app/Screens/PlayingScreen/useBackgroundCues.ts`.
- **`backgroundSession.ts`** (expo-notifications):
  - **Session:** an Android ongoing (`sticky`) notification on a silent LOW-importance channel `workout-session`. It is re-posted under the same identifier, so Android updates it in place, and only when the visible text changes. The text is the workout name, `Exercise:  lap/laps`, and "until HH:MM:SS" (when the current segment ends), or "· paused" / "Workout complete". **On iOS the session calls do nothing**: iOS has no ongoing notifications, and a Live Activity would need native code. The old `progress` field is accepted but not drawn, because a frozen JS thread can't refresh a per-second progress bar; `endsAt` stays correct instead.
  - **Cues:** local notifications at the next boundaries (at most 20; iOS allows 64 pending). Texts look like "Next: Squats", "Squats: lap 2/3", "Rest is over, tap play after your set" and "Workout complete".
    - They are rescheduled on every clock change: start, pause, resume, skip, previous, reload, resync, and edits to the workout. Each boundary has a stable id (`workout-cue-<fromSegment>`), and every reschedule first cancels all pending cues and dismisses delivered ones, so there are no duplicates.
    - A notification handler hides cues while the app is in the foreground (the player plays its own cues there), and keeps the session notification in the tray with no banner or sound.
    - All calls go through one serial queue. Pending cues left over from a killed run are also cancelled.
    - Android: one HIGH-importance channel per (sound, vibration) pair, e.g. `workout-cues-bell-vibrate`, created on first use, because channel sound and vibration can't change after creation. The sound is the user's selected cue sound.
    - iOS: the selected sound's `.caf` copy (iOS can't play mp3 notification sounds), `interruptionLevel: 'active'`.
  - **Permission:** `requestNotificationPermission()` runs when the player opens. It first shows a short Alert explaining why, then the system prompt (Android 13+ POST_NOTIFICATIONS / iOS alert+sound). It asks at most once per app run and never after the system says it can't ask again. On Android it creates the channel first, which Android 13 needs before it will show the prompt.
  - The old `linkingURI 'yourSchemeHere://chat/jane'` is dropped: tapping the notification opens the app, where the player is still mounted. The `ic_launcher` icon option is gone too (see Known issues).
- **`app.config.ts`, expo-notifications options:** `color: '#3c8739'` (the dark theme primary), plus `sounds` (the 4 cue mp3s from `app/Assets/sounds/` for Android res/raw, and 4 IMA4 `.caf` copies in `app/Screens/PlayingScreen/notificationSounds/*_cue.caf` for iOS, made with ffmpeg at 22 kHz mono, 10–38 KB each). The `.caf` files use different basenames so they don't clash with the mp3s in res/raw. Prebuild confirmed all 8 files in `android/.../res/raw` and in the iOS project.

SOUND_CUE_DURATION_MS`. This compiles before and after A3's branch is merged. Same intent as A3's commit 51bceeb.

  - **Please decide on `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM`.** Without it, Android 12+ schedules date triggers with inexact `setAndAllowWhileIdle`, and a locked phone can deliver cues late. It is only a few seconds in light doze, but can be minutes in deep doze. With it, expo-notifications uses `setExactAndAllowWhileIdle`. On Android 14+ that permission is denied by default and the user has to allow it in system settings. `USE_EXACT_ALARM` is restricted by Play policy to alarm/calendar apps, so it is probably not eligible. The on-screen timer is correct either way; this only affects *notification* timing.

  - Notification icon: Android needs a monochrome white-on-transparent PNG (about 96×96) for `expo-notifications.icon`. None exists in the repo. Ask Dmytro for one. Until then Android uses its default small icon, which may look like a plain square.

### Acceptance criteria: all **need a device test** (Dmytro)
1. **Locked-phone accuracy:** needs a device test. Build a workout of about 10 minutes from timed exercises (no repeats, e.g. 30 s × several laps). Start it, lock the phone for 3 minutes, then unlock. Check that the exercise, the lap and the remaining seconds match a stopwatch to within 1 s, on Android and iOS. Repeat with the app sent to the background instead of locking. Also try a workout with a strength exercise (repeats): after unlocking, the player should be **paused** at that exercise's lap, waiting for play.
2. **Notifications while locked:** needs a device test. On the first open of the player, check that the explanation Alert appears and then the system prompt (Android 13+, iOS).
   - Locked: one notification should arrive at each segment boundary, with the selected cue sound (Settings → sound) and vibration when it is on.
   - Pause and resume, or skip, then lock again: there should be no duplicate or stale notifications.
   - While the app is open: no notification banners should appear, only the in-app sound and TTS.
   - Android: the "Workout in progress" notification should update in place (no re-alert) as exercises change, with "until HH:MM:SS".
3. **Cleanup:** needs a device test. Finish a workout, and also leave one midway with Back → confirm. In both cases the tray should hold no workout notifications and nothing more should fire afterwards (wait past the next boundary).
4. **Results:** needs a device test. Change weight/repeats during a workout and save. The saved plan/workout should hold the same values as before the migration. Also check a workout that finished while the phone was locked: on unlock you should see the review with "Save Result", and saving should work.
5. **Foreground cues:** needs a device test with A3's services merged. Check the cue sound at the end of each segment, then the TTS of the next exercise name after the sound ends, the spoken "10, 3, 2, 1" countdown, the vibration patterns, and the sound plus "Workout complete" at the end. All should behave as before. Also check skip, previous, pause and reload, and that the countdown circle stays in step with the time text after unlocking.

## Known issues / follow-ups
- **iOS has no ongoing notification.** The seam is a no-op there. A Live Activity (ActivityKit, native target) would be the iOS equivalent; that would be a separate project.
- **Android notification timing** depends on exact-alarm permission (see the A2 request). The on-screen timer doesn't.
- **Cue volume in notifications** follows the system notification volume, not the in-app cue volume setting.
- **TTS doesn't run in the background.** The notification title stands in for the spoken exercise name.
- If the user turns vibration on or off, or changes the cue sound, Android creates a new channel for that combination. Old channels stay in system settings, at most 8.
- `setNotificationHandler` is global. If another feature adds notifications later, extend the handler in `backgroundSession.ts` instead of setting a second one.
- iOS `inactive` (Control Center, the notification shade) pauses the UI tick. Cues delivered during that time are shown as notifications, and the player re-syncs when the app becomes `active`.
